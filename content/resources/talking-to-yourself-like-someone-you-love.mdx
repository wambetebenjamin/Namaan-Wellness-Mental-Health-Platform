Notice, the next time you make a mistake, what you say to yourself.

Now imagine saying exactly that sentence, in exactly that tone, to a friend who had made the same mistake.

Most people find the comparison uncomfortable immediately. We routinely speak to ourselves with a harshness we would consider cruel if directed at anyone else.

## What self-compassion is not

It is worth being clear, because this is often misunderstood:

- It is **not** self-pity. Pity says *poor me*. Compassion says *this is hard, and hard things are part of being human.*
- It is **not** letting yourself off. Self-compassionate people take *more* responsibility, not less — because they can face a mistake without being destroyed by it.
- It is **not** self-esteem. Self-esteem is a judgement of your worth, often compared to others. Compassion does not require you to be better than anyone.

> Self-compassion is not a reward for doing well. It is what you offer when you have not.

## Why the harsh voice feels true

Many people resist self-compassion because the critical voice seems *useful*. If I am hard on myself, I will not become complacent. I will do better next time.

It is worth testing this honestly. For most people, harshness does not produce better work. It produces anxiety, avoidance, and procrastination — because if trying is this dangerous, it is safer not to begin.

Encouragement is not soft. It is more effective.

## Three parts

**1. Mindfulness.** Notice the difficulty rather than being swept away by it or pretending it is not happening. *This is a hard moment.*

**2. Common humanity.** Remember that failing, struggling and getting things wrong are not personal deficiencies. They are what being a person involves. You are not uniquely broken.

**3. Kindness.** Respond to yourself as you would to someone you love who was in this position. Not with flattery — with warmth and accuracy.

## A practice to try

When you notice the harsh voice, try this:

> *This is hard right now. Other people feel this way too. May I be kind to myself in this moment.*

That is it. It will feel awkward at first. It usually does — particularly for people who have spent years believing they do not deserve it. Awkward is not the same as wrong.

## Where this is hardest

Self-compassion tends to be most difficult precisely where it is most needed: after a genuine failure, when you have hurt someone, or when you are grieving.

Those are the moments the harsh voice is loudest. They are also the moments when you most need someone on your own side — and you are the person who will be with you for all of it.

## If this is hard to do alone

Some people find self-compassion almost impossible at first, particularly those who were criticised harshly growing up or who have experienced trauma. That is not a personal failing. It is often a sign that this is worth working through with someone.
