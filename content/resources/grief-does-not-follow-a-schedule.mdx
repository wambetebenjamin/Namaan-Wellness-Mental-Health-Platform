One of the unhelpful ideas about grief is that it has stages you progress through in order, arriving eventually at acceptance like a destination.

It is a comforting idea because it promises an end. But it was never meant to describe what grief is actually like, and people who believe it often end up thinking they are grieving incorrectly.

You are not behind schedule. Grief is not a project with a deadline.

## What grief is actually like

More often, grief behaves like weather. It comes in waves. Some days are ordinary and then a song, a smell, or an ordinary Tuesday undoes you entirely — often months or years later, and often when you had begun to think you were managing.

This is not regression. It is what grief does.

People also describe:

- **Forgetting and then remembering.** The first few seconds of waking, before memory arrives, and then it all comes back.
- **Physical symptoms.** Exhaustion, appetite changes, aches, a peculiar heaviness. Grief is physical, not only emotional.
- **Anger.** At the person who died, at the hospital, at God, at friends who said the wrong thing. Anger is a normal part of love interrupted.
- **Relief.** Especially after a long illness or a difficult relationship. Relief does not cancel love.

> You are not moving on from someone. You are learning to carry them differently.

## On the things people say

Most hurtful comments come from people who care and do not know what to say. *At least they are at peace.* *Everything happens for a reason.* *Be strong for your family.*

You are permitted to not find these helpful. You are permitted to say so, or to say nothing at all.

## What actually helps

- **Let people do things.** People want to help and often do not know how. Let them bring food, sit quietly, handle a phone call.
- **Keep something ordinary.** One regular thing — a walk, a market, a class. Not to distract yourself, but to have a place that is just yours.
- **Mark the dates.** Anniversaries and birthdays often hurt more than expected. Plan for them rather than being ambushed.
- **Say their name.** People sometimes avoid mentioning the person who died for fear of upsetting you. Most people who are grieving want the opposite.

## Grief that is not death

Loss of a marriage. A pregnancy. A friendship. A home, a country, a body that used to work differently, a future you had planned. These deserve grieving too, and they are often grieved without anyone else recognising that a loss occurred.

## When to reach for more support

Consider speaking to someone if grief has gone on for a long while and you feel stuck in it, if you are unable to manage daily life, or if you are having thoughts of wanting to die.
