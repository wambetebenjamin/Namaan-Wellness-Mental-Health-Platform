The standard advice for stress is to reduce your load: delegate more, say no more often, sleep more, exercise.

This advice is not wrong. It is just often not *available*. You may be caring for parents and children at once. You may be the only income in your household. You may be in a job where "do less" is not an option you can pick.

When advice cannot be followed, it stops being advice and starts being another thing to feel bad about.

## Two kinds of load

It helps to separate your load into two piles:

**The load itself** — the actual demands on your time and energy.

**The load about the load** — the worrying about it, the resentment, the mental rehearsal of conversations, the checking, the believing you are the only one who is struggling with this.

You often cannot change the first pile this week. The second pile is where there is usually room to work, and it can be surprisingly heavy.

> Much of what exhausts people is not the work. It is carrying the work twice — once in reality, and once in their head.

## Three questions worth sitting with

**What am I doing that only I believe is required?** Many people are operating rules they have never examined: that a good parent never says no, that a serious professional replies within the hour, that a person who asks for help is a burden. Which of your rules would you enforce on someone you love?

**Where am I spending energy I am not choosing to spend?** Not the obligations — the things you do automatically. The group chat. The refreshing. The mental argument with someone from three years ago.

**What is one thing I could put down for a week?** Not forever. For a week. Often the discovery is that nothing breaks.

## On rest

Rest is not only sleep and it is not only stopping. There are several kinds, and being depleted in one does not get fixed by another:

- **Physical** — sleep, stillness, gentle movement
- **Mental** — breaks from concentrating and deciding
- **Sensory** — quiet, less screen, less noise
- **Emotional** — a place where you do not have to manage anyone else's feelings
- **Social** — time with people who do not need anything from you

Which of these have you been short of? The answer is often not the obvious one.

## A small practice

At the end of each day, write down one thing that went better than expected. Not a gratitude list, not toxic positivity — just one accurate observation. Over a few weeks, it gently corrects the mind's habit of recording only what went wrong.
