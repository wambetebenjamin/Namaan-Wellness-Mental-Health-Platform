Couples often arrive able to recite their argument perfectly. It goes like this, then you say this, then I say that, and then we are both furious and neither of us remembers how it started.

Almost always, the surface topic is not the actual subject.

## The cycle, not the culprit

Couples therapy does not decide who is right. It looks at the *cycle* — the pattern that repeats between two people regardless of who starts it.

A very common one:

1. One partner feels disconnected and reaches out — sometimes as criticism.
2. The other feels inadequate and withdraws to avoid making it worse.
3. The silence confirms the first partner's fear that they do not matter.
4. They reach harder. The other withdraws further.

Both people are protecting themselves. Both feel alone. Neither is the villain.

> The question is never "whose fault is this?" It is "what are we both afraid of right now?"

## What is underneath

Underneath most recurring conflict sit a small number of fears:

- *Do I matter to you?*
- *Am I safe here, or could you leave?*
- *Does what I need count?*
- *Am I failing you?*

When a couple can name the fear instead of repeating the complaint, the whole conversation changes. "You never help" becomes "I am scared I am doing this alone." That is a very different sentence to be on the receiving end of.

## Three practical shifts

**Describe, do not diagnose.** "You always dismiss me" tells someone who they are. "When I raised it and the conversation moved on, I felt dismissed" tells them what happened and what it did.

**Repair early.** You do not have to resolve a conflict to interrupt it. A hand on the arm, "I do not want to fight with you," or "can we start that again" can stop a cycle in ten seconds.

**Assume the good version.** Most of the time your partner is not trying to hurt you; they are managing their own fear badly. Assuming this is not naive. It is usually accurate.

## When couples therapy helps

Couples come for all sorts of reasons — recurring conflict, an affair, years of distance, co-parenting strain, or a decision about whether to stay together.

It is worth knowing that:

- Therapy is not only for relationships in crisis
- It works even if only one partner is certain about continuing
- It is not the therapist's job to keep a relationship together. That is yours. Therapy makes the decision clearer.
- Some couples use it to separate well, especially where children are involved

## When it is not appropriate

Couples therapy is not the right space where there is ongoing violence, coercion, or fear of a partner. In those circumstances, individual support and safety planning come first. Please tell us if this applies and we will help you find the right support.
