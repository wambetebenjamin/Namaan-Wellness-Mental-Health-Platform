Nearly everyone who comes to me about burnout has already tried the obvious thing. They took leave. They went to the coast. They slept.

And for a few days it lifted — and then, often on the second day back at work or even before, it returned completely.

That experience is one of the clearest signs that this is burnout and not ordinary tiredness.

## What burnout actually is

Burnout has three parts, and tiredness is only one:

- **Exhaustion** that rest does not reliably reverse
- **Cynicism or distance** from work you used to care about
- **Reduced efficacy** — the sense that nothing you do is good enough, however hard you try

It is a state produced by prolonged conditions, which is why a fortnight away does not undo it.

> Burnout is not a personal resilience failure. It is what happens when a nervous system stays switched on for too long.

## Why your body stays switched on

When demands are sustained and your control over them is low, your stress response stops switching off properly. You become adapted to operating in a state that was only ever meant to be temporary.

This is why the common symptoms are physical as well as emotional:

- Waking tired after a full night's sleep
- Getting ill more often than usual
- A tight chest or jaw you only notice when you stop
- Needing caffeine to start and alcohol to stop
- Being unable to properly arrive home — still working, mentally, at 9pm

## Three things that genuinely help

**1. Find the real drain.** Burnout is rarely about hours alone. It is usually about something specific: no control over your work, unclear expectations, a values conflict, or a relationship with one particular person. Name the actual thing before you try to fix "stress."

**2. Finish the stress cycle.** Your body needs physiological completion — movement, breathing with a long exhale, crying, laughter, real connection. Rest alone does not always give it. A walk often does more than an hour on the sofa.

**3. Put a boundary somewhere small.** Not a dramatic resignation. One small, specific, defensible boundary you can actually hold: no email after 7pm, one meeting declined, lunch away from your desk. Small boundaries held reliably change more than large ones abandoned.

## For managers

Your team's burnout is usually visible in behaviour before anyone names it: the careful one making uncharacteristic errors, the reliable one going quiet, increased sick leave, humour turning sharp.

The most useful thing you can do is not a wellness app. It is asking one honest question and being able to hear the answer: *what is getting in your way right now?*

## When to get help

If you have felt this way for more than a few months, if it is affecting your relationships or your health, or if you are using alcohol or work itself to cope — that is the point at which talking to someone helps.
